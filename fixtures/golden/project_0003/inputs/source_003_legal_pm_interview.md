**Interviewer:** Can you describe how legal review fits into the procurement approval flow?

**Legal PM:** Sure. Any contract with a non-standard indemnity, liability, or IP assignment clause requires legal review. That part's in the policy. What the policy doesn't say — but what we've practiced for the last three years — is that our review is a **blocking gate**, not advisory. If we flag a clause as unacceptable, the deal doesn't move forward until it's renegotiated or escalated to general counsel.

**Interviewer:** The finance PM I spoke with earlier said legal is advisory.

**Legal PM:** I disagree. There's no written authority override — we haven't documented a block-power formally anywhere — but in practice we've held the line since the 2023 incident. A finance-approved deal that we flagged got rolled back by GC. Since then, finance approvals on non-standard-clause contracts wait on our sign-off.

**Interviewer:** Do ops have a seat in the approval flow?

**Legal PM:** Not for contract terms. For operational impact — tooling dependencies, on-call effects — ops should have a consult role but the policy gap is real. Right now ops finds out after the decision.

**Interviewer:** How long does legal review typically take?

**Legal PM:** Five business days on a first pass. Another three if the vendor pushes back on our markup. So the "legal adds two days" claim you might have heard is wrong for anything substantive.
